<!--more-->

## Tutorial Sequence

Students work through the tutorials below in groups of 3-4, alongside interactive lectures and readings.

{{% steps %}}

### Polarizers

[{{< icon name="document" >}}PDF](/tutorials/01-Polarizers-Tutorial.pdf)
&nbsp;&nbsp;
[{{< icon name="brands/microsoftword" >}}Word](/tutorials/01-Polarizers-Tutorial.docx)

Students explain the polarization state of a photon as a superposition state using a hidden-variables model. They calculate the probability of transmission, and predict the polarization of a transmitted photon.

### Splitters

[{{< icon name="document" >}}PDF](/tutorials/02-Splitters-Tt.pdf)
&nbsp;&nbsp;
[{{< icon name="brands/microsoftword" >}}Word](/tutorials/02-Splitters-Tt.docx)

Students explore how Copenhagen-type models describe a superposition of states with color/hardness splitters. They apply the idea that measurement reduces a superposition state to a definite state, and predict the possible outcomes and their associated probabilities.

### States

[{{< icon name="document" >}}PDF](/tutorials/03-States.pdf)
&nbsp;&nbsp;
[{{< icon name="brands/microsoftword" >}}Word](/tutorials/03-States.docx)

Using the common ground of hidden-variables and Copenhagen-type models, students apply the mathematical formalism of two-state systems to polarizers, color/hardness splitters, and spin.

### Operators

[{{< icon name="document" >}}PDF](/tutorials/04-Operators.pdf)
&nbsp;&nbsp;
[{{< icon name="brands/microsoftword" >}}Word](/tutorials/04-Operators.docx)

Students represent all spin bras and kets in matrix notation and derive the matrix representation of the projection operator and spin operators.

### Dynamics

[{{< icon name="document" >}}PDF](/tutorials/05-Dynamics.pdf)
&nbsp;&nbsp;
[{{< icon name="brands/microsoftword" >}}Word](/tutorials/05-Dynamics.docx)

Students analyze the time evolution of the spin state of an electron in an external magnetic field.

### Measurement

[{{< icon name="document" >}}PDF](/tutorials/06-Measurement.pdf)
&nbsp;&nbsp;
[{{< icon name="brands/microsoftword" >}}Word](/tutorials/06-Measurement.docx)

Students explore how environmental interaction (decoherence) can lead to a macroscopic object being in a definite state, with or without dynamical collapse of the wavefunction.
